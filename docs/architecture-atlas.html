How Wisplight is put together, and how a story runs through it

Wisplight is a single-player text role-playing game in which every villager is driven by rules first and a language model second. One headless engine sits under two applications: the game you play and the editor you build worlds with. This atlas shows the parts, how they talk, how a world is authored, and how three stories actually unfold through the machinery.

The functional design and the world book in Claude Docs are the specification; this page is a picture of what is built. Where they disagree, the design documents win. Numbers marked "measured" come from the mock model on the fixed test set.

Two applications, one engine

Everything that decides what happens lives in src/engine, which imports nothing from Electron, React or Node. The game window, the conversation window, the editor, the terminal client and the browser preview are all thin shells around it. That is why the same world plays identically in tests, in the editor's playtest and on screen.

Playing

npm run dev opens the game. You type commands or plain sentences; a conversation opens its own window. Time runs in real time while you read and pauses while you type. Every input, output, world event and model reply is written to the game log, so a run can be replayed exactly with the same seed and without calling a model again.

Building

npm run editor opens the editor on any world folder. Places and people have forms; everything else is YAML with a check. A save first loads the whole world with the change and writes only the entity that changed, so comments and layout survive. A running game in the same world picks the change up at once.

The systems, and what each one owns

Each system owns one kind of state and one kind of decision. The colour of the tag says who decides: rules, the AI within bounds, or the content author.

rules

World and clock

Places are a graph with exits and minutes, objects with affordances (what they let you do, what they consume and produce), services with stock and prices that follow stock, and a thirteen-month calendar. Routes are Dijkstra over exits; a flood can close an exit.

world.ts · content.ts · clock.ts · items.ts

rules

NPC life

Every person has needs (hunger, rest, social, safety, work, faith), a profession schedule and daily goals. A planner turns "produce bread" into steps by searching backwards through affordances: buy flour, or grind rye at a mill that must not be broken. Without any model, the village runs on this alone.

npc/brain.ts · npc/planner.ts · npc/execute.ts · npc/acts.ts

AI, bounded

Goal choice (the brain)

Two to four times a game day a small model picks 1 to 3 goals from a fixed catalogue of 29, using only ids the person knows. A validator throws out the rest; behaviour gates (temper, honesty, attitude) decide what a person may even attempt. Nothing waits for the model.

npc/goals.ts · social/gates.ts

rules

Knowledge and news

Whether someone knows a topic is a fixed roll per person and topic, by fame and distance. Facts are written once with three versions (precise, village, far) and spread from person to person where people meet, losing precision, and are forgotten by importance. Since M8.1 a fact can carry a claim that people believe or doubt.

dialogue/knowledge.ts · news.ts

AI, bounded

The voice

One model call per conversation turn, after rules have filtered injection, recognised the topics and built a knowledge packet of only what this person knows. The reply is JSON: speech, names used, topics mentioned, one small relation effect, a memory note. Invented names are rejected; a stock line stands in when the model fails.

dialogue/conversation.ts · dialogue/prompt.ts · dialogue/guard.ts

AI, bounded

The chronicler

At 04:00, or at once for big news, the strong model gets only the storylines that changed, in a compact keyed notation, and writes lore with three versions, keeps a note per storyline, turns open threads into checkable requests, leaves thoughts on people, writes the news of the day, and after big events proposes an effect plan. Templates stand in without a model.

src/chronicler/* · chronicler.ts · storylines.ts

content

Story patterns and pacing

Five small patterns (something lost, a quarrel, a theft, a fever, a feast) start by themselves at a rate set by the tempo you chose (calm, normal, dramatic), more often after a quiet spell. Each plays out in beats and leaves facts and requests behind.

stories.ts · content/base/data/patterns.yaml

content

Quests as storylets

A written quest is data: how it starts, stages with conditions, actions the player can do in plain words, and at least three endings. Conditions come from one shared language (flags, knowledge, attitude, clocks, places, deaths). Quests react to the world: a death or a flooded place moves them on.

quests/schema.ts · quests/engine.ts · content/base/data/quests.yaml

content

Signals and aftermath

Watchers in the content turn a change of state into a signal (a wedding, a vacancy, an empty house, peace). The standard aftermath for that signal is a plan of steps over days with conditions on what people know and verbs that change lasting things: ties, homes, work, households. Every step is news.

rules

The player in the world

Relations with four numbers and an attitude band; deeds, gifts, debts and crimes with witnesses; thirteen factions with reputation; companions with loyalty, orders and their own quest; romance and marriage that runs through the same aftermath as anyone's. The world acts on you as much as you act on it.

Rules and combat

A slim d20: four attributes, skills with practice marks, six classes with talent trees, patrons with favour, conditions, a bestiary and encounters, all in YAML. A world without a rules file plays with a ready-made traveller and no fights, as Skerrow does.

rules

Map and distance

A region is a zone drawing that becomes a hex map with a fixed seed. You see only what you have seen; a place you only heard of is a zone that shrinks as more people describe it. People far from you become notes that become people again when you come near.

map/* · lod.ts · outlines.ts

rules

Persistence and replay

A savegame is the state object plus the log. Loading an older save starts a branch; the later history stays. The layer (what changed about people in play) sits over the content, so the content itself is never touched by play.

What happens in one game minute

The clock advances one minute at a time. Most minutes almost nothing happens; the expensive work is spread over the quarter hour, the hour and the day, and the model runs in the background while the world goes on.

The AI decides, the systems execute

Three roles, three models you choose yourself in the settings, one gateway with budgets. Every role gets a fixed part the provider caches and a changing part with only what this call needs. Every reply passes a validator, and every role has a fallback so the game keeps playing offline.

The gateway

  • One complete(request) door for every role. The request names the role; the settings say which provider and model that role uses.
  • An hourly and a monthly budget in dollars. Low-priority calls (the chronicler, goal choices of people without a quest role, pictures) wait once 80% of the hour's budget is spent, so conversations keep their share.
  • A provider's rate limit pauses only that provider; three failures in a row give a two-minute cool-down for voice and brain calls.
  • Keys are entered by the player, encrypted with the OS keychain, and never logged. The AI log keeps every prompt and reply for the developer view.
  • A price table per model turns tokens into cost; the status bar shows the session's spend, the usage screen the day and month.

Choosing models

  • After connecting a key, an advisor model of that provider recommends a model per role from the ids that key can use, with the price table in its prompt so it does not guess prices.
  • A trial runs the fixed test set (conversation situations, goal choices, chronicler runs) against a model and reports validity, latency, tokens and cost per hour.
  • The exact model id is stored; at start-up the game checks the id still exists.
  • Pictures of places and people are optional, made once by an image model and kept on disk.

How the world knows things, and gets them wrong

No model is needed for news. A fact is written once by the rules with three versions; who knows it, how well and how surely is a table that changes as people meet. When a fact carries a claim, the same table says what someone believes, and a plan can wait for that belief.

What someone knows before anything happens

Every topic has a fame 0 to 5 and a place. A fixed roll from the game seed, the person and the topic is compared with a chance from a table of fame against distance (same settlement, 10 km, 30 km, 100 km, farther). Profession, quirks and age modify it: the innkeeper hears more, the baker knows flour. The margin under the chance sets the level: 1 a rumour, 2 as the village tells it, 3 the whole story. Far places are known only as a direction and days on foot.

Three layers of truth

  • Flags are truth for quests and plans, never doubted.
  • Claims on facts are knowledge: they spread, fade, and can be wrong or lied.
  • Place states and the layer are truth about the world: flooded, occupied, who lives and works where now.

Rule of thumb for authors: anything people could doubt or lie about is a claim; anything only the quest needs to know is a flag.

Signals and aftermath: the loop that makes change last

Since M8.1 the engine has one generic loop next to the fact-to-lore chain. A watcher sees a change in state and raises a signal; the cheapest handler that can take it makes a plan; the simulation works the plan off over days; every step is a fact, which can raise the next signal. The code knows no wedding or war, only signals, conditions, verbs and plans.

A watcher and its aftermath, as content

# watchers.yaml: a fact with the claim key "married" is a life event
- id: wedding
  signal: life_event
  event: wedding
  fact: { key: married }

# aftermath.yaml: what always follows, also without AI
- id: wedding
  signal: life_event
  event: wedding
  topic: household
  steps:
    - { id: tie,      do: { set_tie: [$a, $b], role: spouse, bond: 3 } }
    - { id: home,     after: tie,
        do: { join_household: { mover: [$a, $b] }, of: { stayer: [$a, $b] } } }
    - { id: in_laws,  after: tie, do: { regard: [{ family_of: $b }], to: $a, affinity: 10 } }
    - { id: expects,  after: home, when: [{ is_player: $mover }],
        do: { expect_home: $stayer, of: $mover, nights: 5 } }
    - { id: work,     after: home, wait: 12,
        when: [{ has_work: $mover }, { commute: $mover, at_least: 30 }],
        do: { quit_work: $mover } }

What the loop already does

  • Two villagers get engaged in a quest outcome → a feast on the first rest day a week on, near the one who moves, with both families → the feast records "married" → the wedding aftermath: spouses, one household, in-laws warm up, and the one who now walks half an hour to work quits after a night.
  • Quitting records a vacancy with a claim → a notice on the nearest board, a thought for the innkeeper, and hire waits for someone without work who heard of it and lives within reach.
  • The player's marriage runs through the same aftermath as anyone's.
  • Refugees return only when they believe the war is over and think their house stands; the chronicler's plans get a return step per refugee automatically.
  • A house nobody has lived in for three days becomes news and a free house.

New in M8.2 to M8.4, per the design: intentions the brain chooses for one person or household, standing (five classes), lies with a motive, doubt and asking around, feuds, openness of a settlement, forgetting and recognising; then the chronicler planning for groups; then newcomers, a ledger per settlement and building projects.

Building a world and its stories

A world is a folder of YAML. You add depth in layers: a place to stand in, people with a life, things to know and talk about, small stories that start by themselves, written quests, and finally the events whose consequences the world should carry on its own. Each layer is optional; Skerrow stops after quests and plays fine.

Anatomy of a quest (a storylet)

- id: a_boat_and_a_bride
  kind: social
  givers: [npc_wouter]
  starts: { talk: [npc_wouter], when: [{ attitude: npc_wouter, at_least: Friendly }] }
  stages:
    - { id: asked, text: "Wouter wants to marry Geesje, but Trijntje …",
        next: [{ when: [{ flag: trijntje_blesses }], to: blessed }] }
  actions:
    - { id: plead, say: ["^plead .*trijntje"], intent: "ask Trijntje for her blessing",
        with: npc_trijntje, check: { skill: persuasion, dc: 16 },
        effects: [{ set: trijntje_blesses }], text: "…" }
  outcomes:
    - { id: wed, when: [{ flag: trijntje_blesses }],
        effects: [{ fact: { title: "…", claim: { subject: npc_wouter, key: betrothed, value: npc_geesje } } }] }
    - { id: eloped,   solution: true, when: [...] }
    - { id: too_late, solution: false, when: [{ dead: npc_wouter }] }

The outcome's fact carries a claim; the betrothal watcher sees it and the aftermath takes over. The quest never scripts the wedding.

What a new kind of event needs

You wantYou writeCode?
A new place, person, thing, tradeYAML in its list, or the formno
A rumour going round at the startnews.yaml with who knows itno
A small story that starts by itselfa pattern of an existing kindno (a new kind: yes)
A quest with endingsquests.yamlno
A consequence that unfolds over daysa watcher and an aftermathno
A disaster with phasesplans.yaml, or let the chronicler planno
A new verb (something the world cannot change yet)—yes, once, for every event
A new kind of state (a settlement's ledger)—yes, once

The working agreement in CLAUDE.md: a new kind of event is content, not code. Code only for a new verb or a new kind of state, usable by every event.

Detail by distance

The world is worked out to the level the moment needs. Near you, every minute counts; further off, a coarser tick; far away, a note. Beyond the map, a far city is an outline the chronicler writes once when you set out for it.
