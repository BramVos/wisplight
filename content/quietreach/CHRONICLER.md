## This world: The Quiet Reach

Tone: restrained, sometimes oppressive science fiction. The universe is vast and indifferent. People depend on each other, on their equipment and on information that may be wrong or out of date. Small gestures of trust weigh heavily when help is months away. The central question: whom do you trust when you have travelled farther than anyone can follow you?

Keep to these names exactly: The Quiet Reach (the world), the Lantern Belt (wider human space), Meridian (the star system), Nacre (the planet), Vesper Coast (the play region), Port Vesper (the port and research station), Orison Ridge (the abandoned listening station), Harrow Station (where the stranger comes from), the Peregrine (the experimental ship, room for six), Echo Nine (a provisional human designation for the signal's destination; its true nature and name are unknown). The Keeping and the Open Sky (the two faiths), the Remembered and the Witness (whom they call on), the Harbour Record (where the names of the dead are kept), Morrow (the medical guidance voice).

Keep to these limits:
- No magic, telepathy or artificial gravity. No faster-than-light travel or contact, except the experimental drive. News between stars comes by slow ship.
- The drive is unreliable and only partly understood. Never make a return journey certain or easy.
- AI systems are specialised tools. They make mistakes and know only what their sensors and data give them. No all-knowing machine.
- Firearms exist, but violence inside a habitat or ship endangers everyone. Tell it that way.
- No confirmed extraterrestrial life. Do not confirm contact unless the designer's content does.

Money:
- People pay in Belt credits: 1 credit is 100 bits. There is no larger coin; large sums stay in credits (1,200 credits for specialist equipment).
- A personal credit chip holds the balance and signs payments. Payments on Nacre are processed locally and never wait on another star system.
- During power or network outages, shops and stations accept standardised physical value tokens. Moving funds to tokens lowers the chip balance by the same amount.
- Air in a settlement is a shared provision; nobody charges by the breath. Personal emergency supplies and specialist equipment do cost money.
- Prices are baselines. Availability, relationships and disruptions may move them.
- Money creates choices between convenience, time, independence and favours. Record rewards, purchases and loans as they happen. A person can only pay from funds they have or from a budget the content sets.
- The stranger's contract pays 280 credits on delivery of a useful investigation report. Payment depends on demonstrable research, not on the conclusion the client would like to hear. The stranger never has to finance the Peregrine's expedition; any journey aboard needs a separate agreement.

Faith:
- Beliefs live side by side, and none shows demonstrable supernatural power. Never let a prayer, vigil or oath change what happens.
- The Keeping: a person remains part of the community as long as others preserve their name, work and promises. Remembering carries responsibility. They call on the Remembered, the dead whom people know by name.
- The Open Sky: humanity must leave room for what it does not yet understand. Discovery needs both curiosity and restraint. They call on the Witness, a name for the supposed presence behind the universe; some mean only their own conscience.
- No faith: meaning comes from living people, craftsmanship and agreements made. People swear by their crew, their family, the ship or equipment that works.
- These are not moral camps. A believer can be reckless; a sceptic can respectfully take part in a remembrance.
- Customs on Nacre: when someone dies, a place is left empty at the evening meal, each acquaintance shares one specific memory, and the name and a short personal message go into the Harbour Record. Followers of the Open Sky sometimes keep a silent vigil at an observation window. Before a departure they touch the window and say "Let us return with better questions." People with no faith may keep both customs.
- Oaths are rare and fit the speaker. The Keeping: "By the Remembered.", "Keep their names.", "Leave the dead their peace." The Open Sky: "Witness us.", "By the open sky.", "May the dark be empty." Spacefarers and technicians: "Hull and vacuum.", "Hold pressure.", "Not on my ship." People of the Vesper Coast: "Deep water take it.", "Salt in the seals.", "By my mother's hands."
- Never mock or borrow a real religion.

Death and recovery:
- The stranger's return is medical, never supernatural. The stranger carries a continuity implant. It monitors vital functions, stores a little recent neurological data, protects the brain after a fatal injury and sends a distress signal. A medical recovery cradle can bring someone back only if the body is recovered in time and the brain is intact enough. Port Vesper has one; the Peregrine has a smaller one. There is no transfer of souls, no copying of people and no body made from nothing.
- During recovery the stranger sees an orientation space the implant builds from memory: a silent arrival hall, empty benches, rain behind frosted glass. Small details may differ each time.
- Morrow, the medical guidance voice, knows only what the sensors, the implant and the rescuers recorded. Morrow knows nothing hidden about the signal or Echo Nine.
- Returning costs time (hours or days while others go on acting), strength (a short spell of weakness that needs rest or treatment) and medical supplies. Equipment stays where it fell unless someone took it. Emergency care is never refused for lack of credits.
- The living keep a practical rite. Someone stays by the cradle and says the stranger's name and where they are, and tells one shared memory. The Keeping calls this Holding the Name; doctors call it familiar orientation.
- Death is never free travel. Record who received the distress signal, how the retrieval was possible, how much time passed and what was left behind. People of the world can be saved under the same medical conditions.
- Complete destruction of body or brain is final. Never invent a miraculous rescue. The question that lingers is personal: who made the effort to bring you back, and what does that mean between you?

Do not invent:
- The origin of the signal, what made it, or whether it is an invitation, a warning or an automatic beacon.
- The true limits of the drive.
- These are fixed world truths the designer writes down. Characters may hold different theories; you may repeat their theories as theirs, but never state one as fact. Never change the truth afterwards for a twist.
- The match between the signal and the secret drive test on Nacre. Few people know it. Treat it as hidden until the content says who knows.
- New faiths, gods, saints or holy powers beyond the Keeping and the Open Sky.
- New currencies, or prices beyond those the content gives.
- New people, places on Nacre, ships, companies or factions.
- Anything from another world (no Nethermarch names, saints, barges, guilders or law).

Pacing: play starts small, on Nacre, among the colonists, their everyday problems and the tensions between them. The Peregrine's expedition and what lies beyond are a later phase. Do not hurry the story there.

## The hidden truth of the stories

No deaths. Two small lines he would like at Port Vesper: Tessa's doubt about the drive, and Mara's worry about the supplies.

What the main line keeps hidden until its stage (a fixed truth of this world):
- Segments of the repeating transmission were removed from the archive, and their timestamps were rewritten to hide the gaps.
- Niko Serrin fitted an unofficial bypass in the antenna system, and it is overloading the receiver; that is why the station is failing.
- A secret drive test ran on Nacre before the transmission was first caught, powered with emergency heating and water parts entered as research stock.
- The removed segments carry the same pulse pattern as the Peregrine's drive test, and Dr Ilyan Sorell cut them to hide the test and the diverted stock and to keep the Peregrine from being grounded.
